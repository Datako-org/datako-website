SMEs & Data organisation

Building a data team inside a smaller company.

A company's first data hire often shapes the next five years. Here are the trade-offs that come up most, and the ones that cost most when they are settled badly.

14 May 20266 min read

The single all-rounder is a dead end

The usual reflex is to look for one person who can hold the infrastructure, produce the analysis and build the models. That profile exists, but it is rare, expensive, and above all cannot excel at everything at once: engineering and analysis are not practised at the same rhythm or on the same objects.

The real risk is not a failed hire. It is dependency: after two years one person knows the calculation rules, and their departure stops the company.

Start from the need, not the org chart

Before defining a role, it helps to answer three questions: what is missing today in order to decide, how often that gap shows up, and who will use the result. The answer points to very different first hires.

A company that cannot consolidate its figures has an engineering problem. A company that has the figures but draws nothing from them has an analysis and delivery problem. The two are hired differently.

  • The figures exist but will not come together → engineering need.
  • The figures come together but nobody uses them → analysis need.
  • The figures circulate but nobody knows which are right → governance need.
Hire for the problem you have this year, not for the organisation you hope to have in five.

What must exist before the second hire

Three things stop a new team from becoming a dependency: business definitions written somewhere other than in someone's head, code versioned in a shared repository, and a known procedure for taking over if a pipeline stops.

These are not large-company requirements. They are what separates an installed capability from personal knowledge.

In-house, outsourced, or both

Some skills are better kept inside: those touching the business, the definitions, the trade-offs. Others are usefully rented at the start, when the need is one-off or highly technical — setting up ingestion, framing governance, industrialising a model.

The reasonable split is usually that one: keep the business knowledge, buy the technical acceleration — and write down what was done, so you do not buy the same thing twice.
